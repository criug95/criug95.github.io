I want to understand how massive stars, their binary companions and the star clusters they live in shape the population of black holes we observe with gravitational waves. During my PhD at SISSA I worked on single stars and binary populations. At GSSI I model populations of both isolated and dynamically formed binaries.

How massive stars collapse
======

The mass of a black hole depends on how its progenitor explodes, or fails to. I computed the remnant masses of stars between 13 and 120 solar masses, at different metallicities and rotation velocities, exploding them with the hydrodynamical code HYPERION calibrated on SN 1987A ([Ugolini et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202451483)). I then implemented these results in the SEVN population synthesis code.

With Francesco Gabrielli I work on pair-instability supernovae, which set the upper edge of the black hole mass spectrum ([Gabrielli et al. 2024](https://doi.org/10.1093/mnras/stae2048); [Gabrielli, Ugolini et al. 2026](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/202661687)).

Black hole binaries from isolated stars
======

Using SEVN and POSYDON I study where the features of the observed binary black hole mass distribution come from, and which of them binary evolution alone can explain. The first paper of this series, *Piling up in the darkness*, is in preparation.

Black holes in star clusters
======

I am one of the core developers of [B-POP](https://github.com/marcasedda/BPOP), a semi-analytical code for black hole binaries formed in young, globular and nuclear star clusters. My main contribution is a formalism that follows the secondary branches of hierarchical merger trees. These branches populate the high-mass, high-spin tail of the binary black hole population and are one of the routes to intermediate-mass black holes. I also used these models to interpret GW231123, the most massive binary detected so far ([Paiella, Ugolini et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.3847/2041-8213/ae1447)).

What comes next
======

Common-envelope evolution is still the weakest link in binary models. I want to test it against luminous red novae, the transients produced when a common envelope is ejected, and I co-supervise a PhD thesis on luminous red nova rates with SEVN. I am also interested in close encounters between stars and black holes in dense clusters, including tidal disruption events.
